Simulation and validation

What planted concepts, borrowed observation models and a withheld pairing establish, and what they do not.

What this produces

Collections in which named concepts are known to carry a phenotype contrast. With them: a score for how much of that planting the estimator gave back, and a placement of concept-space recovery between the best any method could reach and alignment at random.

The modalities are measured on different individuals, so what connects them is a fixed vocabulary of named biological concepts. A concept is a set of features that belong together for a stated biological reason, and every modality is scored on the same concepts. A validation therefore has to answer two questions that no real cohort can. Are the concepts that move with the phenotype the ones that get called? And does a concept scored in one modality track the same concept scored in another?

What a simulation has to reproduce, and what it does not

The estimator reads a normalised feature-by-sample matrix. Fragment bias, retention time, ionisation efficiency and isotope envelopes are modelled upstream of that matrix, and normalisation removes or absorbs them before anything reaches the estimator. The simulation target is therefore not measurement physics but the six properties of a prepared matrix the estimator can respond to:

  1. the per-feature marginal distribution, including its skewness and tail weight;
  2. the mean-variance relation across features;
  3. missingness as a function of abundance;
  4. the feature-feature correlation structure, summarised by its eigenspectrum;
  5. non-Gaussianity relevant to the recovery of free dimensions;
  6. the design margins, including unpopulated cells and covariates recorded in only one cohort.

A generator that reproduces these properties provides a useful observation-model stress test. Which properties of a real assay it does not capture bounds what recovery on it establishes.

Three questions, three constructions

Planting concepts

chorale_concept_example() builds a collection in which named concepts carry a phenotype shift. The vocabulary, how many of its concepts are planted, how many features a modality measures of each, and the size of the shift are all arguments.

library(chorale)

fx <- chorale_concept_example(n_samples = 60, n_features = 200,
                              n_concepts = 6, n_planted = 2,
                              effect = 0.1, coverage = 1, seed = 1)
fit <- chorale_concept_fit(fx$containers, fx$sets, n_free = 0,
                           n_permutations = 999)
chorale_score_concepts(fit, fx$planted)$summary

chorale_score_concepts() answers two questions rather than one. Recall is the share of planted concepts that were called. The false positive rate is the share of the rest of the vocabulary that was called as well. A method that calls everything answers the first and fails the second, so neither is the result on its own.

Rank AUC is the probability that a planted concept outranks one that was not planted. A threshold is a choice and a ranking is not, so the ranking says how far the two are separated whatever cut is applied. Chance is 0.5.

Which control decides a call is an argument, because the two answer different questions. criterion = "fdr" holds the share of calls expected to be false at the configured level; criterion = "family_wise" holds the probability of any false call anywhere in the vocabulary.

chorale_plant() plants concepts into the observation model of real layers instead. The planting and scoring vocabularies are the same one, because a concept is planted and recovered by name: the question is whether the design shows it changing with the phenotype, not whether its composition can be rediscovered.

Recovery curves

chorale_validate_concepts() runs a grid of regimes, draws several collections from each, and averages the recovery over them. Three dials matter and each asks a different question.

grid <- data.frame(label = c("0.20", "0.10", "0.05", "0.02", "0.00"),
                   effect = c(0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.02, 0),
                   n_concepts = 6L, n_planted = 2L,
                   n_features = 200L, n_samples = 40L)
chorale_validate_concepts(grid, n_rep = 5, n_permutations = 999)
#>   label effect recall false_positive_rate rank_auc n_evaluated
#> 1  0.20   0.20    1.0                0.05    1.000           5
#> 2  0.10   0.10    0.4                0.10    0.825           5
#> 3  0.05   0.05    0.1                0.00    0.675           5
#> 4  0.02   0.02    0.0                0.00    0.600           5
#> 5  0.00   0.00    0.0                0.00    0.550           5

Recall falls away between a per-feature shift of 0.2 and one of 0.05 standard deviations, in forty samples per cohort with concepts of about thirty features. The false positive rate stays at or below the level it was called at throughout, and at a shift of zero the ranking sits at chance, which is what a calibrated null looks like from this side.

Effect size asks how strong a concept’s phenotype contrast has to be. The unit is the shift in each member feature, not in the concept score, and the difference matters. A concept score is a mean over its members, so a shift of ddd in each of mmm members that are otherwise independent moves the score by about dmd\sqrt{m}dm​. Concept scoring aggregates, which is why per-feature effects far below the threshold of a per-feature test are recovered.

Sample size asks whether bulk cohorts are large enough. Vocabulary coverage asks how far the vocabulary has to reach into a modality’s measured features, which is the question an incomplete annotation raises. A concept defined on fifty features but measured on twelve is scored on the twelve.

A regime the estimator cannot be run in is reported with its reason rather than as an empty cell. A coverage low enough that no concept reaches the minimum number of features leaves nothing to score, and that is a result about the regime.

Giving the simulation the shape of real data

chorale_data_profile() measures the six properties from any matrix and returns them as one object. It stores summaries rather than data: no sample identifier enters it, and no covariate left unnamed.

profile <- chorale_data_profile(assay = my_matrix, col_data = my_design,
                                covariates = c("phenotype", "sex"),
                                layer = "PROT")

Passing the profile to chorale_simulate() replaces the observation model with it and leaves the latent structure untouched. Each simulated feature is read through a real feature’s quantile grid, so the values that come out have the distribution a real feature had. The map is monotone, so the sign and the ordering of a planted design response survive it. The design is drawn from the cells the real cohort populated, so a cell no sample occupied stays empty.

Correlation enters separately, as directions drawn at random in feature space with Gaussian scores whose variances follow the profile’s eigenvalues, scaled until the leading direction carries the share of variance the profile records. Those scores are Gaussian, so they add covariance without adding anything a decomposition driven by non-Gaussianity could recover as a source.

chorale_profile_agreement() places a simulated matrix beside the real one on the same six properties, which is what a claim of realism is bounded to.

Recovery against a withheld pairing

Where two modalities were measured on the same individuals, the pairing can be withheld and used afterwards. chorale_destroy_pairing() presents the two modalities under their own sample identifiers, encodes them as though disjoint, and only then uses the pairing to ask whether a concept scored in one tracks the same concept scored in the other.

r <- chorale_destroy_pairing(paired_a, paired_b, design, sets = sets,
                             n_random = 200)
r$concept_summary
#>   n_concepts paired_upper_bound recovered_agreement random_baseline
#> 1          4             0.8827              0.8827          0.2713
#>   placement_between_bounds fraction_partner_correct
#> 1                        1                        1

Three quantities frame the result. The paired upper bound is the best alignment the pairing admits, which is what any method knowing the pairing could reach. The random baseline is the mean over one-to-one relabellings of the concepts drawn at random. The recovered agreement is the identity the vocabulary asserts, concept for concept by name, computed without a paired sample ever having been seen. A placement of 1 means the identity is the correspondence the pairing shows.

The agreement is a signed correlation, because a concept score has a fixed orientation. A larger score means a larger weighted mean of the features carrying the concept, so two modalities that rank the same state in opposite directions have not recovered it. Random alignment is therefore a baseline and not a bound. A vocabulary whose names line up while the biology does not places below it, and the placement reports that as a negative value rather than clipping it to zero.

Per-concept results say where that holds and where it does not.

r$concept_recovery
#>   concept self_agreement best_partner best_agreement partner_correct
#> 1    beta         0.9764         beta         0.9764            TRUE
#> 2   alpha         0.9739        alpha         0.9739            TRUE
#> 3   gamma         0.9737        gamma         0.9737            TRUE
#> 4   delta         0.6067        delta         0.6067            TRUE

The benchmark takes any two modalities measured on the same individuals and any vocabulary, so it is a property of the estimator rather than of one collection.

Validating families and joint transfer

Concept recovery alone does not validate the newer group and joint analyses. Their simulations need distinct targets.

For a concept family, plant modest concordant shifts across several overlapping concepts and compare them with families whose members point in opposite directions. Recovery means the concordant family statistic gains power while the discordant sum cancels, with family-wise error controlled under a complete null. Because the definitions overlap, every member must reuse the same signed permutation draw; independent null draws would be an invalid benchmark.

For a joint state, plant a low-rank pattern over several concept columns in every modality, then assess four properties separately:

  1. reconstruction over observed entries;
  2. loading stability across starts and sample perturbations;
  3. calibrated phenotype evidence on the fitted scores; and
  4. leave-one-modality-out prediction in a modality excluded from fitting.

The fourth is the strongest test of cross-modality portability. It still does not prove a component is a unique latent mechanism: sign, order, and rotations remain non-identifiable, and more than one low-rank basis can describe the same reconstruction. Simulations should therefore compare loading subspaces or matched directions, not raw component labels.

Coverage missing by modality must also be planted explicitly. Joint-state selection currently uses a zero-filled matrix for reproducibility selection and the observed-entry mask for fitting. A validation should vary that coverage pattern so selection artefacts are not mistaken for biological rank.

Conditions of applicability

Recovery measured under borrowed marginals holds for marginals resembling the profiled ones. A modality whose distribution or missingness differs materially requires its own profile.

A concept reaches a modality only through the identifiers or classes that modality carries. A lipidome resolves at the level of lipid class, not lipid species, and a metabolome with no class mapping cannot be scored on a pathway vocabulary.

The effect size a curve is drawn against is a shift in each member feature. A concept score aggregates its members, so the same curve read as though the unit were the concept score would overstate what is being asked of the data by about the square root of the concept’s size.

Correlation outside the planted concepts is reproduced as its eigenspectrum, through directions drawn at random in feature space. Those directions carry no biological grouping, so a modality whose dominant axis has a biological identity is reproduced in the share of variance that axis carries and not in which features it uses.

Concept-space recovery measured against a withheld pairing is measured where a pairing exists. Whether it holds in a collection assembled from separate deposits is what the pairing benchmark bounds rather than what it establishes.
