Why CHORALE exists

What can be learned when omics modalities were measured on different individuals.

CHORALE is for collections in which each omics modality was measured on a different set of individuals. It does not invent sample pairs. It changes the question to one the observed data can answer.

What is missing

If one cohort supplies a transcriptome and another a proteome, there is no row describing the same person twice. An individual-level joint profile is therefore not merely unknown: it is not observed and cannot be recovered without additional assumptions or paired bridge data. Methods that require matched rows, shared features, or a translation learned from paired examples do not apply directly.

The common reference frame

CHORALE connects modalities through a vocabulary of named biological concepts. A concept is a feature set chosen for a stated reason, such as a pathway or a cell-type signature. Every modality is scored on the same vocabulary before the phenotype is consulted.

This produces a common table shape: samples by concepts. The measurements that created the scores may be different, but each column has a declared biological meaning. Coverage remains explicit because a modality contributes to a concept only when it measures enough of its members.

Two questions from one encoding

The common encoding supports two complementary analyses.

Concept evidence asks a prespecified question. Within each modality, does a named concept change with the phenotype after adjustment for shared covariates? The adjusted effects are combined by inverse-variance weighting. This is a meta-analysis: each modality estimates an effect that it could have estimated alone, and integration improves the evidence when modalities agree.

Joint-state analysis asks a multivariate question. Do several concepts vary together across the stacked collection? CHORALE standardises concept scores within modality, optionally removes modality-local nuisance effects, and fits a low-rank representation over the observed entries. The resulting components are directions through concept space shared by construction. They are fitted summaries, not uniquely identified mechanisms.

Three safeguards against easy stories

Concept families test whether overlapping concepts move together without pretending they are independent. Leave-one-modality-out transfer asks whether a joint direction estimated from the other modalities predicts phenotype in a cohort that contributed none of its samples or assay structure. Free dimensions retain coordinated variation that the vocabulary cannot name.

These answer different questions. A concept can be supported without belonging to a strong joint component; a joint component can be significant without every modality carrying it equally; and a free dimension can be real while remaining biologically unnamed.

What significance means

The design is held fixed during calibration. CHORALE fits the reduced model without the effect under test, exchanges its residuals, and rebuilds the score. This Freedman—Lane procedure preserves the observed relation between phenotype and adjusting covariates. Family-wise p-values protect against any false call in the tested family, while q-values estimate the false-discovery proportion among calls. Neither is a causal claim.

What a result can establish

A supported concept is an adjusted phenotype association in a named piece of biology, measured in the modalities listed in its row, at a stated error rate. A transferred joint component is a multiconcept direction fitted without the held-out modality that still predicts its phenotype. Neither result reconstructs an individual’s missing modalities or orders cause and consequence.

An absence of significant rows also has several meanings. The analysis may not have been supplied, the quantity may not have been estimable, or it may have been tested and failed to beat its null. CHORALE reports these states separately because only the last is a statistical negative result.
