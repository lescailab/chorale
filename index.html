R package · disjoint-cohort multi-omics

Omics measured on different individuals, integrated through named biology.

Measuring several omics layers on the same individuals is rarely possible: the assay consumes the sample, each platform needs its own preparation, and the layers of a study usually come from different groups at different times. CHORALE scores every modality on one fixed vocabulary of named biological concepts, then asks two questions of it. Whether the design of each study supports a named concept, and what low-rank state is supported by the collection as a whole.

Run the tutorialWhy it existsSource

No individual appears twice, and the cohorts need not be the same size. Two modalities need share neither an individual nor a feature to be compared on how far a named piece of biology moves with the phenotype in each of them. The concept scores then branch. On the left each modality is read separately and the answers combined, so what is estimated is a quantity a modality could have estimated alone. On the right every sample is stacked against every concept and fitted once, which estimates directions from more samples and broader concept coverage than any single modality contributes.

What it gives you where the same individuals were never measured twice

01

A concept is the same concept everywhere

Its name and its members are fixed before the data are seen, so the modalities meet in one space and no correspondence between them has to be estimated.

02

A few hundred stated hypotheses

Each modality is asked whether a named concept changes with the configured phenotype, under family-wise control across the whole vocabulary and a false discovery rate estimated over the same permutations.

03

Biology outside the vocabulary

The concept scores are removed from each assay and what remains is decomposed into free dimensions. A free dimension that moves with the phenotype is coordinated variation the vocabulary has no name for.

04

A state estimated from the collection

The concept scores of every modality share their columns, so they stack into one matrix of every sample against every concept and are decomposed once. The direction that comes out uses the samples and observed concept coverage of the whole collection. It is a fitted low-rank summary, not a uniquely identified mechanism.

05

Tested where it was not estimated

Each modality is held out, the state is estimated from the rest, and the held-out cohort is projected onto loadings carrying none of it: not its samples, batches, assay or preparation. This tests portability beyond the assay structure used for fitting.

06

Concepts tested as groups

A change spread across a dozen overlapping concepts can leave every one below threshold while the group is unambiguous. Families are formed from the vocabulary before anything is tested, and scored on the directional sum across their members.

07

Coverage before results

chorale_concepts() reports how many features each modality has for each concept before anything is fitted, so the size of the claim is known in advance.

08

A calibration that keeps the design

The null holds the design fixed and exchanges the part of a score the covariates do not explain, and one permutation serves every concept, so concepts sharing features stay dependent in the null as they are in the data.

What a result looks like

One row per concept, carrying the modalities that express it, the effect combined across them, and the error rates the vocabulary was read at. This is the first table the report writes, as concepts.tsv.

| concept | modalities | joint_z | sign_agreement | p_family | q_value | |---|---|---|---|---|---| | REACTOME_COMPLEMENT_CASCADE | RNA, PROT, METAB | 5.12 | TRUE | 0.004 | 0.01 | | HALLMARK_OXIDATIVE_PHOSPHORYLATION | RNA, PROT | -4.03 | TRUE | 0.031 | 0.04 |

p_family is read against the largest statistic anywhere in the vocabulary, so it accounts for every concept the run scored rather than the ones it reported.sign_agreement says whether the modalities moved the same way: a concept whose layers disagree in direction has not been recovered in common, whatever the size of its effects.

Alongside it, chorale_concept_specificity() puts each covariate the modalities share in the phenotype's place, on the terms that covariate contributes, which separates a concept that tracks the phenotype from one tracking something the cohorts differ in.

What the collection knows and its members do not

Combining per-modality effects is a meta-analysis: the modality is the unit of estimation, and what is estimated is a number each modality could in principle have estimated alone.chorale_joint_state() estimates something else. Every modality is described on the same vocabulary, so the score matrices share their columns and stack by row into one matrix of every sample against every concept, decomposed once.

The result is a fitted direction over the whole vocabulary. Stacking increases the number and diversity of observed rows and allows concepts with incomplete coverage to contribute where they are measured; it does not make an individual component uniquely identifiable. A concept a modality cannot express is missing rather than zero: after centring within modality, zero would be a claim that the modality measured exactly average, so the entry carries no weight instead and the narrowest layer never bounds the vocabulary.

| held out | trained on | component | loading_agreement | z | p_value | |---|---|---|---|---|---| | RNA | PROT, LIPID, METAB | joint_04 | 0.91 | 5.97 | 0.0002 | | PROT | RNA, LIPID, METAB | joint_05 | 0.88 | -6.21 | 0.0002 |

chorale_joint_transfer() holds each modality out, estimates the state from the rest, and projects the held-out cohort onto loadings containing none of it. A permutation asks whether structure could arise by chance under this design; transfer asks whether it survives in a cohort sharing no individual, batch, assay or preparation with the one that produced it. It is evidence of portability beyond modality-specific structure, while remaining an association rather than a causal claim.

Built for the data that exists

Deposited cohorts

Public transcriptomes, proteomes and lipidomes for one condition, generated by different groups on different individuals.

Two columns required

A sample identifier and a phenotype. Every other column is a covariate, used where the modalities share it and dropped where they do not.

Tens to hundreds of samples

Statistics estimable at the cohort sizes bulk multi-omics resources actually reach, with precision that grows with samples rather than with design strata.

The whole workflow

Three matrices, three sample tables, one call. chorale_load() returns aSummarizedExperiment per modality, and everything after the fit reads results rather than configuring the method.

containers <- Map(chorale_load, matrices, designs)

concepts <- chorale_concepts(containers,
                            sets          = chorale_genesets(species = "Mus musculus"),
                            feature_space = c(METAB = "lipid"))

fit <- chorale_concept_fit(containers, concepts, n_free = "auto")

fit                                   # the concepts the design supports
chorale_free_dimensions(fit)          # what moved with the phenotype unnamed
chorale_concept_specificity(fit)      # whether another covariate does the same

families <- chorale_concept_families(concepts)
family_result <- chorale_family_evidence(fit$evidence, families)

state <- chorale_joint_state(fit$encoding, nuisance = list(RNA = "study"))
joint_result <- chorale_joint_evidence(state)
transfer <- chorale_joint_transfer(fit$encoding)

chorale_report(
  fit, chorale_null(fit, containers), "results/",
  family_evidence = family_result,
  joint_state = state,
  joint_evidence = joint_result,
  joint_transfer = transfer
)
